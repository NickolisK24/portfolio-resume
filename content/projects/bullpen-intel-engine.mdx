## Why I Built It

Baseball has always been the industry I wanted to work in. BaseballOS
started as an attempt to combine software engineering, data analysis, and
baseball decision-making into a system that emphasizes trust,
explainability, and defensible recommendations over black-box outputs.

## Problem

Bullpen management is one of the highest-leverage decisions a coaching
staff makes, but the signal is scattered across pitch counts,
back-to-back appearances, workload trends, rest windows, freshness, and
confidence. I wanted BaseballOS to collapse that noise into a defensible
decision-support layer instead of a raw stat table.

Built around league-wide MLB data ingestion with automated
synchronization, availability analysis, fatigue modeling, and
recommendation workflows.

## Architecture

Five core systems backed by a shared ingestion and trust layer:

1. **Data ingestion** — synchronizes MLB game and player data into a
   durable Postgres model.
2. **Fatigue engine** — quantifies recent workload risk using
   appearances, pitch counts, and recovery windows.
3. **Availability engine** — classifies pitchers into Monitor, Limited,
   Avoid, and Unavailable categories.
4. **Recommendation engine** — produces explainable pitcher
   recommendations with fail-closed eligibility gates.
5. **Trust and transparency layer** — surfaces freshness, confidence,
   limitations, and recommendation reasoning directly in the UI.

## Key engineering decisions

- **Fail-closed recommendations** — the system refuses to recommend when
  identity, freshness, confidence, or availability requirements are not
  met.
- **Policy separated from scoring** — recommendation behavior is governed
  by explicit eligibility rules instead of hidden ranking assumptions.
- **Trust metadata travels with the output** — freshness, confidence,
  limitations, and reasoning remain visible to the user.
- **Configurable thresholds** — workload and availability rules can
  evolve without hardcoding every decision.

## Engineering Challenges

- Designing recommendation outputs that fail closed instead of guessing.
- Keeping trust metadata attached to every recommendation and UI state.
- Separating policy decisions from scoring logic so recommendation
  behavior remains auditable.
- Making freshness and sync status visible instead of silently trusting
  stale data.

## Current Capabilities

- Automated MLB data ingestion and synchronization
- Bullpen fatigue scoring and workload analysis
- Availability classification across Monitor, Limited, Avoid, and
  Unavailable states
- Explainable pitcher recommendations with eligibility gating
- Freshness and trust transparency throughout the dashboard
- Historical workload and bullpen decision-support workflows

## Status

Deployed and actively evolving. Current focus is expanding pitcher search,
improving bullpen decision workflows, and preparing the recommendation
system for broader ranking and selection logic.
